A skill for Claude Code

Decks you click through

Describe a talk and Claude builds it as one HTML file: a fixed stage where every press reveals the next piece, and a quiet loop marks whatever you are talking about. No build, no framework, no network. It runs from a double-click on a borrowed laptop, ten minutes before you speak.

What it is for

When the picture does the explaining

Slides are pages you flip. A stepped deck is one scene that builds while you talk, so the room sees the thing you are describing, in the order you describe it.

  • Roadmaps and timelines

    Milestones that land one at a time, so the date arrives with its reason.

  • Flows and architecture

    Boxes and routed wires, with a pulse that travels the connection you are on.

  • Decisions

    Options against criteria, narrowed down in front of the people who have to agree.

  • Stories and journeys

    A road wider than the screen, with a camera that follows you along it.

  • Walkthroughs and portfolios

    Work laid out as a set, with detail panels for the pieces someone asks about.

  • Teaching

    A concept built up in steps you can go back over, and print as a handout afterwards.

Themes and motion

Pick a look, pick a pace

How a deck looks and how it moves are chosen separately, so any theme works with any motion style. Or hand Claude a link, a brand PDF or a path into a repo, and it derives a theme from that, fixing any colour too faint to read on a projector.

Themes

House
Condensed capitals, mono for anything that is data, cool paper.
Editorial
A serif display in sentence case, warm paper, one deep accent.
Swiss
White, black and one red. Square corners, strong rules.
Midnight
A dark stage with a warm accent, for rooms with the lights down.
From a reference
Matched to your site, brand guide or design tokens.

Motion

Calm
Soft fades and a small rise. Nothing competes with the speaker.
Crisp
Fast, clean wipes. No bounce, no travel.
Playful
Cards pop out of the marker they belong to and overshoot a little.
Drawn
Lines draw on, titles type in, the camera takes its time.

How to use it

Install, ask, present

  1. Install the skill

    Clone it into your Claude Code skills folder. Claude picks it up in the next session, and loads it whenever you ask for something you will click through or present.

    git clone https://github.com/jonasster/stepped-presentations ~/.claude/skills/stepped-presentations

  2. Ask for the deck

    Say what it is for, when you present, and what goes in it. If that is all there, Claude builds the whole deck and lists the choices it made for you. If the content is not settled yet, it asks one short round of questions, each with a default, then stops at a few checkpoints so you can steer.

    “I present our 2026 roadmap at the all-hands next week. Six milestones, appearing one at a time as I talk. It has to run off my laptop; the meeting room wifi is terrible. Swiss theme, Crisp motion.”

    It works on decks you already have, too: add a step, fix a connector that runs through a label, embed screenshots, or make a deck self-contained before you email it.

  3. Present it

    Double-click the file. Everything you need is on the keyboard:

    → Spacenext step ←back, exactly the way you came R Freset, or show the whole slide Ppresenter view: your notes, the next step and a timer in a second window Shift Pa PDF with one page per step, or a handout with your notes

  4. Fine-tune without a prompt

    Nudging a colour, fixing a typo or trying another easing is not worth a conversation. The studio opens a deck folder in Chrome or Edge, runs your deck beside an outline and an inspector, and writes each change back into your source one value at a time.

    python -m http.server 5180 --directory studio

Built in

What every deck gets

  • One file, offline

    Fonts and images can be inlined, so the deck runs from a USB stick or an email attachment.

  • Back is free

    The whole scene is derived from one step number, so ←, deep links and jumping around always land right.

  • Presenter view

    Notes, the next step and a timer in a second window. Drive it from either one.

  • PDF and handouts

    One page per step at full size, with transitions off so nothing prints halfway.

  • Content as data

    What you say lives in arrays at the top. Adding a milestone is adding an entry.

  • Motion that means something

    Only the current step moves, and every animation has a static version for reduced motion.

  • Checked in a browser

    Claude opens the deck and measures it before calling a visual change done.

  • Yours to edit

    Plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript. No framework to learn, nothing to install to change it.
